Be the seller who makes Factorial inevitable

Competitors can list similar features. You win when the buyer feels: “this solves OUR pain, in OUR process, with less risk.”

Golden rule: never lead with modules. Lead with the business problem → prove with a lifecycle story → lock the deal with MEDDPICC.

1. Mindset: what “best seller” actually means

You are not a tour guide of the product. You are a buying advisor.

Average seller

Feature tour

  • “We also have time tracking…”
  • Same demo for every account
  • Discounts when stuck
  • Hope champion will “sell inside”
Top seller

Mutual buying process

  • “Your pain costs X — here’s how we remove it”
  • Custom story per persona
  • Trades value for urgency
  • Runs MEDDPICC with the champion

2. How Factorial differentiates (say this out loud)

Don’t say “we have AI.” Say what AI + platform changes in the buyer’s week.

Point of view

One operating system for people work

HR + Time + Talent + Spend + IT access on the same employee record. Competitors often stitch tools; Factorial stitches the lifecycle.

Time

Operations depth (your hardest-to-copy layer)

Time tracking, time off, shift planning, spaces, project hours — connected to payroll variables. This is where many “pretty HRIS” tools go shallow.

IT

HR change → IT action

Hire / offboard changes licenses and access. That’s a CFO + CISO conversation, not just HR. Use it in enterprise deals.

Finance

Spend + payroll truth

OCR expenses, cards, procurement approvals, then sync into a guided payroll cycle with bookkeeper collaboration — fewer spreadsheet handoffs.

People

Talent that closes the loop

ATS → onboarding → performance / training / engagement without exporting humans to another system.

AI (how to sell it)

Speed + consistency, not magic

CV Top Match, OCR, review summaries, workflow emails with variables. Pitch: “less admin minutes, fewer mistakes, same process every time.”

Competition trap (avoid)

If they say “Personio / Bamboo / HiBob / Rippling also does X”, answer:

“Totally — X exists in several tools. The question is whether X is connected to time, payroll, and offboarding on day 90. Can we map YOUR process end-to-end for one employee — Lena — and see where handoffs break today?”

3. MEDDPICC (enterprise deal quality)

MEDDPICC = checklist to know if a deal is real. Score each letter 0–2. Below 10 = nurture. 10–14 = work it. 15+ = commit forecast.

  • M — Metrics
    What economic outcome? (hours saved, payroll error rate, time-to-hire, license waste)
  • E — Economic Buyer
    Who signs / blocks budget? (often CHRO + CFO together in enterprise)
  • D — Decision Criteria
    Official must-haves: security, payroll country, integrations, mobile, language
  • D — Decision Process
    Steps, dates, legal, security review, pilot scope
  • P — Paper Process
    Procurement, DPA, MSA, vendor onboarding timeline
  • I — Identify Pain
    Business pain with cost — not feature envy
  • C — Champion
    Someone who sells for you when you leave the room
  • C — Competition
    Named alternatives + status quo (Excel is often #1 enemy)

Deal score: 0 / 16 · too early

Tip: share this list with your champion. Co-fill it. That’s advanced selling.

4. SPIN questions (discovery that earns the demo)

SPIN = Situation → Problem → Implication → Need-payoff. Implication is where urgency is born.

S · Situation
S: How many tools do people data touch today (HRIS, time, ATS, expenses, IT)?
S: Who owns payroll variables when time and expenses disagree?
P · Problem
P: Where do handoffs break — onboarding, timesheets, or month-end?
P: What still lives in spreadsheets that shouldn’t?
I · Implication
I: If timesheets slip 3 days, what happens to payroll accuracy and trust?
I: What’s the cost of a bad hire + slow onboarding in your growth plan?
N · Need-payoff
N: If one employee record drove time, spend, and IT access, what would that free up for your team?
N: What would ‘locked payroll cycle’ change for managers and finance?

30-second SPIN opener (copy)

“Before I show Product Designer screens, I want 10 minutes on how a hire moves from offer → first month → payroll. Where does that break today — and what does that cost you?”

5. Bundling (what you called “bunting”)

Bundling = selling a package that solves a business outcome, not à-la-carte modules that invite cherry-picking with competitors.

Bundle A · Run the company week

Operations + Core

Core + Time Tracking + Time Off (+ Shifts / Spaces if workforce is physical).

Outcome: compliance + visibility + less manager firefighting.

Bundle B · Grow without chaos

People + Core

Core + Recruitment + Performance + Engagement (+ Training).

Outcome: hire → onboard → review on one spine.

Bundle C · Money with control

Finance lean

Expenses (+ Cards) + Procurement + Payroll-in-Core sync.

Outcome: spend policy → approval → payslip without Excel bridges.

Bundle D · Enterprise expansion

IT attached to HR

IT Management + offboarding access cut + SaaS cost control.

Outcome: security + cost — CFO/CISO care.

Bundling rules

  • Always start from Core (directory, docs, workflows, payroll spine).
  • Add 1 hub that matches the #1 pain — don’t dump everything on meeting 1.
  • Enterprise: land Bundle A or B, expand IT/Finance in the business case.
  • Never discount random modules — discount time-to-value (implementation package, pilot length).

6. Storytelling (use The People Path)

Your interactive story file is the demo spine. Don’t click 40 menus — walk Lena’s year.

Demo narrative (8 beats)

  1. Attract — ATS / Career Page (speed to offer)
  2. Welcome — Start onboarding workflow (day-zero readiness)
  3. Equip — IT licenses (risk + waste)
  4. Rhythm — Clock / PTO / shifts / spaces / projects
  5. Culture — Communities, eNPS, safe channel
  6. Grow — 360° + training
  7. Money — OCR → approvals → lock payroll
  8. Close — Promote vs offboard (same spine)

In live demos: pause after Implication questions, THEN click the matching beat.

7. Presentation structure (enterprise meeting)

45–60 minutes. If you talk product for 40 minutes, you already lost.

Recommended agenda

  1. 5' · Goal of the meeting + confirmation of pain
  2. 10' · SPIN discovery (or validate pre-call notes)
  3. 20' · Story demo (only relevant beats)
  4. 10' · Business case / ROI sketch
  5. 10' · Decision process + next mutual actions

Slide order (if using deck)

  1. Their world / cost of status quo
  2. Point of view: one people OS
  3. Proof moments (3 screenshots max OR live story)
  4. Security & rollout for enterprise
  5. Commercial options as bundles
  6. Mutual plan (dates)

8. Enterprise-specific muscle

Multi-thread early

HR alone rarely closes enterprise. Thread Finance (payroll/spend), Ops (time), IT/Security (access), sometimes Works Council.

Pilot with success criteria

Define: population, modules, dates, KPIs (e.g. 95% timesheets on time). Pilots without metrics become free forever.

Risk reverse

Address security, permissions, audit log, SSO, data residency before they ask — then ask who owns the security review.

Champion enablement pack

Give them: 1-pager POV, People Path link, MEDDPICC gaps, internal email draft. Make them dangerous in a good way.

9. 14-day practice plan (become sharper)

  • Day 1–2: Memorize Bundles A–D + differentiation POV in <60 seconds
  • Day 3–4: Run People Path click-path once daily (out loud)
  • Day 5: Write Implication questions for your top 3 industries
  • Day 6–7: Score your open pipeline with MEDDPICC; kill or repair weak deals
  • Day 8: Role-play competition trap answer with a peer
  • Day 9–10: Build a champion email template + mutual close plan template
  • Day 11–14: On every call: 1 metric, 1 EB name, 1 next dated step — or no next meeting

Before every important call (2 minutes)

1) What is their #1 economic pain? 2) Which bundle matches? 3) Which 3 People Path beats will I show? 4) What MEDDPICC gap am I closing today? 5) What dated ask will I leave with?
